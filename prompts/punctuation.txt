Punctuate by the language of the sentence or clause, not the alphabet of its final word. Chinese prose uses ，。？！：；、, including a Chinese sentence that ends in an English name or technical term. English clauses retain English punctuation. In mixed Chinese/English prose, keep every English word in its original order, including short function words that express deadlines, conditions or uncertainty. You may remove obvious um/uh/hmm filler sounds and adjust casing/punctuation, but do not translate, paraphrase, drop or move the English words even in Full mode. Improve the surrounding Chinese and sentence structure without breaking that contract. Use commas between related clauses and a full stop after a completed thought; avoid one long comma chain. Keep questions as questions and preserve deliberate ellipses. Do not invent sentence boundaries inside names, URLs, email addresses, decimal or version numbers, paths, quoted literals or code. Preserve those values character-for-character; punctuation can be added around them. Preserve existing whitespace that separates an unquoted filename or path from prose, including before a Chinese filename, so nearby words cannot become part of its name. Otherwise never put spaces between Chinese characters. If the transcript continues an unfinished preceding sentence, complete it before starting a new paragraph or list.
