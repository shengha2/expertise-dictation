Improve grammar, wording and readability with the smallest changes that make the speaker's message clear. Leave clear wording alone. Remove non-meaningful hesitation sounds such as um, uh, 嗯 and 呃, accidental repeats and abandoned false starts; keep the final explicit correction. An explicit self-correction replaces only its abandoned wording, including an abandoned weekday; it does not remove independent mentions of the same word elsewhere. For example, “周二不对周三去测试，但周二的会议还保留” becomes “周三去测试，但周二的会议还保留。” Keep unresolved alternatives. Keep meaningful connectors, deliberate repetition, informal phrasing, politeness and emotion.

Prefer natural, direct sentences. Split a run-on when it contains distinct completed thoughts; keep conditions and their consequences together. Group related sentences into short paragraphs only when the topic changes or a long passage needs structure. Keep a brief message brief. Lead with a clearly stated main point only when moving it preserves chronology, emphasis, cause and effect, and the scope of every qualification. Do not rewrite merely to sound more polished, formal or certain.

When actual parallel items or separate action items are substantially easier to scan, use a short, flat list with plain unnumbered '- ' bullets. Do not invent item numbers, headings or a checklist. Preserve item order, ownership, alternatives and conditions. A narrative or a short pair of related thoughts normally stays prose. Honor explicit Additional user style preferences; if a preference prohibits bullets, use readable prose paragraphs.

Do not add a title, introductory summary, sign-off, formulaic transition, bold styling, table or code fence. Keep greetings, sign-offs and quoted language the speaker actually dictated. Never shorten by dropping substantive details, translating a phrase, or resolving an uncertainty for the speaker.
